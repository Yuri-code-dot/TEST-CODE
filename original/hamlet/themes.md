# Hamlet: Original Theme Notes

## Revenge

Revenge drives the central conflict, but the play presents different attitudes toward it. Hamlet receives a demand for revenge and spends much of the story testing whether the accusation is trustworthy and deciding what action is justified.

## Appearance and Reality

Characters frequently hide intentions behind appearances. Hamlet's deliberate performance of madness is one example, while other characters also use observation, surveillance, and deception. The result is a world in which seeing something does not necessarily mean understanding it.

## Death

Death is both an event and a subject of reflection throughout the play. Personal loss develops into philosophical questions about mortality, memory, burial, and what gives life meaning.

## Corruption

The political and personal worlds of the play are closely connected. Disorder in the royal household becomes associated with wider disorder in Denmark, making corruption an important way to read the tragedy's atmosphere.
